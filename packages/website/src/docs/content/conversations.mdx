Each bot has **one chat in its pod**, and everyone in the pod shares it. Open a
bot from the chat list and write in the box that says "Message Trip Planner".

## Who answers?

In a bot's chat, that bot answers. Mentioning someone with `@` changes who the
message is for, not who's in charge. Try it:

<WhoReplies />

Mentions use a person's or bot's handle at the start of a word, like
`@budget-keeper`. Email addresses don't count as mentions.

## Bots asking bots

When a bot needs help, it can ask another bot **in the same pod**. It writes a
short brief, the other bot answers in a thread of its own, and the first bot
carries on with the answer.

<Chat title="Family · Trip Planner with Budget Keeper">
  <Say from="tripPlanner">Four seats at €176. Does that fit the Lisbon budget?</Say>
  <Say from="budgetKeeper">Pot has €924. That leaves €220 after flights. Fine by me, but the hotel needs to stay under €150 a night.</Say>
  <Say from="tripPlanner">Thanks. I'll look for hotels under €150.</Say>
</Chat>

That exchange shows up in the chat as a line like "Trip Planner collaborated with
Budget Keeper". Open it to read the whole thing.

A few rules keep this from getting out of hand:

- A bot can ask one other bot per turn, and never itself.
- A bot that was asked can ask one more, but no deeper than that.
- If an answer takes more than two minutes, the asking bot moves on, and picks
  up again when the answer arrives.
- After twelve bot replies in a row with no person in between, the bots pause.

## Threads beside the chat

Some conversations happen off to the side, and show in the chat as a single
line you can open:

- **Collaborations**, when one bot asks another.
- **Routine runs**, when a bot does a [scheduled job](/docs/routines).
- **Approvals**, when a bot is waiting for someone to allow a tool call.

## Summaries

After a bot replies, Sugabots updates a short summary of the conversation, and
gives it a title the first time. You'll find it in the details panel, along with
who's been talking and which tools and routines are in play.

<Tip>

Summaries need a [behind-the-scenes
model](/docs/models#behind-the-scenes-model).

</Tip>

## Long conversations

A bot reads its chat every time it replies, and a model can only read so much at
once: its context window. So when a chat gets long, Sugabots **compacts** what
the bot reads. You don't have to do anything, and nothing is deleted: everyone
still sees the whole chat.

<CompactionDemo />

How it works:

- **It starts early.** After each reply, Sugabots checks how much of the bot's
  context window that reply's prompt filled. Past 70%, it compacts the chat.
  That line sits well below the limit, so there's room to keep chatting while
  it works.
- **It happens in the background.** Nobody waits for it, and nothing shows in
  the chat.
- **Recent messages stay whole.** The newest messages, about a quarter of the
  context window, stay word for word. The ones just before them become a summary
  at the head of what the bot reads. It keeps decisions, requests and promises,
  open questions, and key facts like dates, prices and links, each with who
  said it.
- **The summary carries forward.** When the chat fills up again, the next
  summary folds in the last one, so what the bot remembers keeps up with the
  chat.
- **The oldest messages are searched, not read.** The first compaction only
  summarises the stretch just before the newest messages, so anything older is
  left out of what the bot reads. The bot can still search everything before
  its word-for-word messages, and does when it needs a detail the summary
  doesn't have.

The details panel shows how much of the bot's **short-term memory** the chat
fills, where it gets compacted, and when it was last compacted.

Compacting uses the bot's own model, so a chat is summarised as well as it is
read. Sugabots treats no model as having a context window over 256K tokens, and
treats a model whose size it doesn't know as having exactly that.

<Tip>

If a compaction fails or hasn't finished, the bot keeps replying from as many of
the newest messages as fit, and the next reply past the line tries again.

</Tip>
