title: Your first workspace

The first time you sign in, Sugabots walks you through five short steps. Only
an admin can finish them, and the first account on an installation is always
the owner of the workspace it creates.

## The five steps

<Steps>

1. Name your workspace

   Something like "The Smiths' bots" or "Acme Support". You can rename it later
   in **Settings → General**.

2. Connect a model

   Bots need a model to think with. Pick **Anthropic**, **OpenAI**,
   **OpenRouter** or **Ollama**, then paste an API key, or give Ollama's address
   if it runs on your own machine. Then choose which of its models your first
   bot uses. You can switch on more, or add other providers, later in
   **Settings → Models**.

3. Make your first bot

   Give it a colour, some eyes and a name, then say what it should help with:
   **Inbox**, **Research**, **Planning**, **Reminders** or something else. This
   bot lives in your Personal pod, so it's yours alone.

4. Bring your friends

   Type in some email addresses and Sugabots sends each person an invite. They
   join as members. Invites expire after two days, and you can **Resend** them
   from **Settings → Members**. Or **Skip for now**.

   <Tip title="Sharing with others">

   If you want to share Sugabots with others, you'll need to get it hosted.
   Running it on your own computer, as in the Quickstart? Skip this step for
   now.

   </Tip>

5. Start chatting

   Your bot is ready and says hello.

</Steps>

## After onboarding

<Tip title="Pick a behind-the-scenes model">

Sugabots uses a [model of its own](/docs/models#behind-the-scenes-model) to
summarise and title conversations. It starts with none. Pick one in **Settings →
Models**, under **Default → System agents use**. A fast, cheap model is best.
Until you do, chats have no summaries or titles.

</Tip>

A few good next steps:

- [Create a shared pod](/docs/pods) for a group, and add people to it once your
  installation is hosted.
- [Make more bots](/docs/bots), each with its own job and model.
- [Plug in a connection](/docs/connections), like Linear or Notion, so bots can
  work with your apps.
