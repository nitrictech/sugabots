A bot is an AI teammate. Each one has its own instructions and model, and its
own chats, files, computer, connectors and automations. Its chats are kept
in a hidden project of its own, which it leads alone, so they work just as a
[project's](/docs/projects) do; only you never see that project itself.

## Make a bot

Owners, admins and editors can make bots. Every member can make a
[Personal Assistant](#personal-assistants). When you make a bot, you choose:

- **Shape**, **Eyes** and **Colour**: its avatar. There are 21 shapes, five
  pairs of eyes and 16 colours.
- **Name**: what members call it. Its handle comes from the name, so
  "Trip Planner" is `@trip-planner`. Names and handles are unique across the
  workspace, but for Personal Assistants': yours are `@personal-assistant`,
  then `@personal-assistant-2` and so on, as everyone else's are, and no other
  bot may take those. A Personal Assistant you make after deleting one may take
  its handle, so an old link to the deleted one opens the new one.
- **Model**: what the bot thinks with. Sugabots suggests the workspace's
  [default model](/docs/models#default-model) when one is set. If no models
  are available, ask the workspace's owner or an admin to enable one in
  **Settings → Models**.
- **Public**, **private** or **personal**: who can talk to it. A public bot
  can be made private later, and a private bot public, but whether a bot is
  personal can't be changed afterwards. See [below](#public-private-and-personal).

A new bot is pinned to your sidebar, and opens in a setup chat: it interviews
you about the work it's for, then asks you to approve the instructions it wrote
for itself. You can read and change them afterwards in its settings. A bot
changes its own instructions only in its own chats, and only once you approve.

## Public, private and personal

- A **public** bot is for everyone in the workspace. Anyone can find it, talk to
  it, and see its chats and files. It can be added to any
  [project](/docs/projects).
- A **private** bot is for its members. They can find it, talk to it, and see
  its chats and files. Viewers can take part in the chats. Members of the bot,
  except viewers, can add or remove workspace members under the bot's **Settings → Members**.
  The owner and admins are members automatically. The creator, once a
  member, stays a member while in the workspace. Removing someone from the bot
  leaves their project memberships in place. The bot can be
  added to [private projects](/docs/projects#public-and-private) only, where
  everyone in the project can talk to it in the project's chats, but not in
  the bot's own chats unless they're its members too. If you made a bot before
  the upgrade to projects, [check the membership exception](/docs/upgrading-to-projects#what-changes).
- A **personal** bot is for its creator alone, and works with their permissions:
  it can reach whatever they can, in every project they're in, and asks them
  before it changes anything. Nobody else can talk to it or see its chats, and
  it can't be shared or added to projects. You manage your own personal bots,
  whatever your role. The workspace's owner and admins can see that it exists,
  delete it or switch off its automations and connectors. Only you can read its
  chats.

The workspace's owner and admins can switch a bot between public and private
in its settings. So can its creator if they can talk to the bot and have a role
other than viewer. A bot made private stays in its private projects and leaves
every public project. If it leads one, choose another lead bot there first.
Making a bot public opens its chats and files to the workspace and clears its
member list. If you make it private again, add its members back.

Whether a bot is personal is chosen when it's made, and never changes: a public
or private bot can't be made personal, and a personal bot can't be made public
or private, since its chats hold what it found with your permissions.

## What a bot has

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Chats | Its chats, each about one piece of work. See [Chats](/docs/chats) |
| Files | Files uploaded or made in its chats |
| Computer | Its own machine, where it runs commands and keeps files in its chats. It sleeps when idle. In a project's chats it uses the [project's computer](/docs/projects#computer) instead |
| Connectors | The services and APIs it works with, and its own GitHub app. In a project's chats it uses the [project's connectors](/docs/projects#connectors) plus any of its own that the workspace's owner and admins have [turned on there](/docs/connectors#a-bots-own-connectors-in-a-project). See [Connectors](/docs/connectors) |
| Automations | Work it does on its own, on a routine, such as every weekday at 9, or when something triggers it. See [Automations](/docs/automations) |

## Tell it what to do

You can change your personal bot's settings whatever your role. For a public or
private bot, the workspace's owner and admins can change its settings, as can
its creator if they can talk to it and have a role other than viewer.

| Setting | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| Description | One line on what the bot is for. Other bots read it to decide whether to ask it for help |
| Model | What it thinks with. Each bot can use a different one |
| Instructions | How it should work. It follows these in every chat and project |
| Tools | Which built-in tools it may use |
| Computer use | Whether it may run commands and browse on a computer. The bot's creator, the workspace's owner or an admin can turn it on in the bot's settings. It works while the workspace has an enabled computer provider |

<Tip title="Writing instructions">

Write them like a note to a new teammate: what it's for, how to sound, and what
to never do. You have up to 20,000 characters. Everyone who can see a bot can
read its instructions, so describe the job, not the member asking.

</Tip>

For example:

```md title="Instructions"
You plan family trips. Find options that suit everyone, and always check the
price with @budget-keeper before suggesting anything.

Keep replies short. Nobody wants to read an essay on a phone.

Never book anything without asking first.
```

## Your sidebar

The sidebar shows the bots you've pinned, under your
[Personal Assistants](#personal-assistants). The rest of the workspace's
bots are a browse away. Pin or unpin any bot you can see, but your Personal
Assistants, which always sit at the top.

## Personal Assistants

A **Personal Assistant** is a [personal bot](#public-private-and-personal) that knows
the workspace. Everyone is given one when they join, and anyone can make more
under **Bots**, say one for each part of their work. Your Personal Assistants
are always first in your sidebar, oldest first, and the workspace opens on the
oldest; if you have none, it offers to make you one. Only you can talk to yours or see their chats,
admins included.

It knows the workspace: the projects, the bots you can reach and what each is
for, and what is waiting on you, such as unread mentions,
approvals and failing automations. Ask it what needs you, or how something in
Sugabots works. It points you to the right bot, and starts a chat with it for
you when you agree. It can also make a project, join one, add a bot to a
project or pin a bot for you: each waits for your approval before it happens, as
any change a personal bot makes does. You manage it as any personal bot of
yours, whatever your role: its name, avatar, model and tools, its connectors, and its automations,
whose runs only you can read and start. Its instructions are looked after by
Sugabots, so you can't edit them, and it keeps its handle and stays personal.

## Remove a bot

**Delete bot** is at the bottom of its settings. Deleting it removes its
chats, files, connectors, automations and everything on its computer. This
can't be undone. If it leads a project, choose another lead there before
deleting it. Delete your last Personal Assistant and you have none until you
make another.
