Conversations happen in threads and direct messages:

- A [project](/docs/projects)'s **threads** are shared by all its people and
  bots. Each is about one piece of work.
- A **bot's threads** are conversations with that one bot. Open a bot and write
  in the box that says "Message Trip Planner" to start one. Every bot has its
  own hidden project for them, which it leads alone, so they work just as a
  project's threads do. Everyone who can see the bot can see its threads and
  join in.
- A **direct message** is between two or more people, with no bots. Only its
  people see it.

Anyone in a thread can mark it done once its work is finished, and it opens
again when someone posts in it.

## Who answers?

Every project has a **lead bot**, and the same rule picks who answers in every
thread:

- **Mention bots** in the project, and every bot you mention answers, all at once.
- **Reply to a bot's message**, and that bot answers.
- **Mention only people**, and the lead bot reads it. The message is most
  likely for them, so it answers only if it can help, and otherwise reacts 👀
  to show it read it.
- **Otherwise** the lead bot answers.

Mentions of bots outside the project are ignored, so in a bot's own threads,
where it's the only bot, that bot answers. Mentioning someone with `@` changes
who the message is for, not who's in charge. Try it:

In a direct message, no bot answers.

Mentions use a person's or bot's handle at the start of a word, like
`@budget-keeper`. Email addresses don't count as mentions.

`@here` and `@channel` mention everyone who can see the conversation, and
notify each of them as a mention does. They are for people: a message with
`@here` or `@channel` is read by the lead bot as one that mentions only people,
unless it names a bot too.
A bot can't use them to notify anyone, and no bot can take `here` or `channel`
as its handle.

## Bots asking bots

When a bot needs help, it can ask **any other bot** in the workspace, public or
private. It writes a short brief, the other bot works on it in a thread of its
own, and the first bot carries on with the answer.

<Chat title="Trip Planner with Budget Keeper">
  <Say from="tripPlanner">Four seats at €176. Does that fit the Lisbon budget?</Say>
  <Say from="budgetKeeper">Pot has €924. That leaves €220 after flights. Fine by me, but the hotel needs to stay under €150 a night.</Say>
  <Say from="tripPlanner">Thanks. I'll look for hotels under €150.</Say>
</Chat>

That exchange shows up under Trip Planner's message as a line,
"Collaboration with Budget Keeper". Open it to read the whole thing.

A few rules keep this from getting out of hand:

- A bot can ask one other bot per turn, and never itself.
- A bot that was asked can ask one more, but no deeper than that.
- If an answer takes more than two minutes, the asking bot moves on, and picks
  up again when the answer arrives.
- After twelve bot replies in a row with no person in between, the bots pause.

## Notifications

Sugabots can notify you in your browser, the way Slack does. Turn them on from
the bar across the top of the app, or in **Settings → Notifications**. Each browser and
device is turned on by itself, and notifies you while Sugabots is open in it,
even in a tab you aren't looking at.

You choose what you hear about:

- **Mentions and replies**, to begin with: messages that mention you, a bot's
  answers to you, replies in conversations you're part of, and approvals that
  wait on you.
- **Everything**: every new message in the conversations you can see.
- **Nothing**.

Unless you chose Nothing, you also hear when a bot can't finish its reply to
you, and when a run of an automation you made or started fails. In **Settings →
Notifications** you can turn the first off, and hear of every run or none.

Right-click a project in the sidebar to give it a level of its own, or to mute
it, for good or, under **More options**, for a while, and right-click a bot to
do the same for its threads. Each follows your own level until you choose
otherwise. You can also pause all notifications for a while, or set quiet hours
each day. You aren't notified about a conversation you're looking at.

## Summaries

After a bot replies in one of a bot's own threads or an automation feed,
Sugabots updates a short summary of the conversation, and gives it a title the
first time. You'll find the summary in the details panel, along with who's been
talking and which tools and automations are in play. A project's threads keep the name you
gave them and aren't summarised, and nor are direct messages.

## Long conversations

A bot rereads the conversation every time it replies, and it can only hold so much in
mind at once. Think of it as the bot's **short-term memory**. When a chat gets
long, Sugabots makes room by compacting what the bot rereads. You don't have to
do anything, and nothing is deleted: everyone still sees the whole chat.

<CompactionDemo />

How it works:

- **It starts early.** Once a chat fills most of the bot's short-term memory,
  Sugabots starts compacting it, while there's still plenty of room to keep
  chatting.
- **It happens in the background.** Nobody waits for it, and nothing shows in
  the chat.
- **Recent messages stay whole.** The bot keeps rereading the latest messages
  word for word. The ones before them become a summary of what was decided,
  asked for and promised, what's still open, and key details like dates and
  prices, along with who said each thing.
- **The summary carries forward.** When the chat fills up again, the new summary
  builds on the last one.
- **The bot can look further back.** The summary can't hold everything. When the
  bot needs a detail it doesn't have, it searches the earlier messages.

To see how full a bot's short-term memory is, and when it was last compacted,
open the chat's details panel.
