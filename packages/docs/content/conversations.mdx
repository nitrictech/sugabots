Each bot has **one chat in its pod**, and everyone in the pod shares it. Open a
bot from the chat list and write in the box that says "Message Trip Planner".

## Who answers?

In a bot's chat, that bot answers. Mentioning someone with `@` changes who the
message is for, not who's in charge. Try it:

<WhoReplies />

Mentions use a person's or bot's handle at the start of a word, like
`@budget-keeper`. Email addresses don't count as mentions.

## Bots asking bots

When a bot needs help, it can ask another bot **in the same pod**. It writes a
short brief, the other bot answers in a thread of its own, and the first bot
carries on with the answer.

<Chat title="Family · Trip Planner with Budget Keeper">
  <Say from="tripPlanner">Four seats at €176. Does that fit the Lisbon budget?</Say>
  <Say from="budgetKeeper">Pot has €924. That leaves €220 after flights. Fine by me, but the hotel needs to stay under €150 a night.</Say>
  <Say from="tripPlanner">Thanks. I'll look for hotels under €150.</Say>
</Chat>

That exchange shows up under Trip Planner's message in the chat as a line,
"Collaboration with Budget Keeper". Open it to read the whole thing.

A few rules keep this from getting out of hand:

- A bot can ask one other bot per turn, and never itself.
- A bot that was asked can ask one more, but no deeper than that.
- If an answer takes more than two minutes, the asking bot moves on, and picks
  up again when the answer arrives.
- After twelve bot replies in a row with no person in between, the bots pause.

## Threads beside the chat

Some conversations happen off to the side, and show in the chat as a single
line you can open:

- **Collaborations**, when one bot asks another.
- **Routine runs**, when a bot does a [scheduled job](/docs/routines).
- **Approvals**, when a bot is waiting for someone to allow a tool call.

## Notifications

Sugabots can notify you in your browser, the way Slack does. Turn them on from
the bar across the top of the app, or in **Settings → Notifications**. Each browser and
device is turned on by itself, and notifies you while Sugabots is open in it,
even in a tab you aren't looking at.

You choose what you hear about:

- **Mentions and replies**, to begin with: messages that mention you, a bot's
  answers to you, replies in conversations you're part of, and approvals that
  wait on you.
- **Everything**: every new message in the conversations you can see.
- **Nothing**.

Unless you chose Nothing, you also hear when a bot can't finish its reply to
you, and when a run of a routine you made or started fails. In **Settings →
Notifications** you can turn the first off, and hear of every run or none.

Right-click a pod in the sidebar to give it a level of its own, or to mute it,
for good or, under **More options**, for a while, and right-click a bot to do the same for it. A bot
follows its pod until you choose otherwise, and a pod follows your own level. A
bot's level covers its conversations and what it posts in the pod's chats, though
a pod muted for a while quiets every bot in it. You
can also pause all notifications for a while, or set quiet hours each day. You aren't notified
about a conversation you're looking at.

## Summaries

After a bot replies, Sugabots updates a short summary of the conversation, and
gives it a title the first time. You'll find it in the details panel, along with
who's been talking and which tools and routines are in play.

## Long conversations

A bot rereads its chat every time it replies, and it can only hold so much in
mind at once. Think of it as the bot's **short-term memory**. When a chat gets
long, Sugabots makes room by compacting what the bot rereads. You don't have to
do anything, and nothing is deleted: everyone still sees the whole chat.

<CompactionDemo />

How it works:

- **It starts early.** Once a chat fills most of the bot's short-term memory,
  Sugabots starts compacting it, while there's still plenty of room to keep
  chatting.
- **It happens in the background.** Nobody waits for it, and nothing shows in
  the chat.
- **Recent messages stay whole.** The bot keeps rereading the latest messages
  word for word. The ones before them become a summary of what was decided,
  asked for and promised, what's still open, and key details like dates and
  prices, along with who said each thing.
- **The summary carries forward.** When the chat fills up again, the new summary
  builds on the last one.
- **The bot can look further back.** The summary can't hold everything. When the
  bot needs a detail it doesn't have, it searches the earlier messages.

To see how full a bot's short-term memory is, and when it was last compacted,
open the chat's details panel.
