There are two places to chat:

- A [project](/docs/projects)'s **chats** are shared by all its members and
  bots. Each is about one piece of work.
- A **bot's chats** are chats with that one bot. Open a bot and write
  in the box that says "Message Trip Planner" to start one. Every bot has its
  own hidden project for them, which it leads alone, so they work just as a
  project's chats do. Whoever can talk to the bot can read and join its
  chats.

Members talk to each other in a project's chats: there are no chats
between members alone.

Anyone in a chat can mark it resolved once its work is finished: it moves
to the Resolved list at the foot of the sidebar, and opens again when
someone posts in it or it is reopened.

## Who answers?

Every project has a **lead bot**, and the same rule picks who answers in every
chat:

- **Mention bots** in the project, and every bot you mention answers, all at once.
- **Reply to a bot's message**, and that bot answers.
- **Mention only members**, and the lead bot reads it if it has a model. The
  message is most likely for them, so it answers only if it can help, and
  otherwise reacts 👀 to show it read it. If the lead bot has no model, the
  message is posted without a bot reply.
- **Otherwise** the lead bot answers.

Mentions of bots outside the project are ignored, so in a bot's own chats,
where it's the only bot, only that bot can answer, by the same rule. Mentioning someone with `@` changes
who the message is for, not who's in charge. Try it:

Mentions use a member's or bot's handle at the start of a word, like
`@budget-keeper`. Email addresses don't count as mentions.

`@here` and `@channel` mention everyone who can see the chat, and
notify each of them as a mention does. They are for members: a message with
`@here` or `@channel` is read by the lead bot as one that mentions only members,
unless it names a bot too.
A bot can't use them to notify anyone, and no bot can take `here` or `channel`
as its handle.

## Bots asking bots

When a bot needs help, it can ask **another of the project's bots** by
mentioning it, as you would. The bot it asks answers right there in the chat,
and once every bot it asked has answered, the first bot picks up again with
what they said.

<Chat title="Lisbon trip">
  <Say from="tripPlanner">Four seats at €176. @budget-keeper, does that fit the Lisbon budget?</Say>
  <Say from="budgetKeeper">Pot has €924. That leaves €220 after flights. Fine by me, but the hotel needs to stay under €150 a night.</Say>
  <Say from="tripPlanner">It fits. I'll look for hotels under €150 a night.</Say>
</Chat>

A bot asks for whoever it's answering: the bot it asks works with that
member's access, and its tool calls are approved as any bot's in the chat
are. In an automation's run, it works for the run, and the run ends once the
bots it asked have answered.

A few rules keep this from getting out of hand:

- A bot can ask only the project's other bots, never itself. In a bot's own
  chats there's nobody else to ask, and in an automation feed a bot's
  mentions bring in no other bot.
- A bot that was asked can't pass the question on: its mentions bring in
  nobody.
- When the first bot picks up again, it can ask bots it hasn't asked yet, but
  not the same ones again.

## Notifications

Sugabots can notify you in your browser, the way Slack does. Turn them on from
the bar across the top of the app, or in **Settings → Notifications**. Each browser and
device is turned on by itself, and notifies you while Sugabots is open in it,
even in a tab you aren't looking at.

You choose what you hear about:

- **Mentions and replies**, to begin with: messages that mention you, a bot's
  answers to you, replies in chats you're part of, and approvals that
  wait on you.
- **Everything**: every new message in the chats you can see.
- **Nothing**.

Unless you chose Nothing, you also hear when a bot can't finish its reply to
you, and when a run of an automation you made or started fails. In **Settings →
Notifications** you can turn the first off, and hear of every run or none.

Right-click a project in the sidebar to give it a level of its own, or to mute
it, for good or, under **More options**, for a while, and right-click a bot to
do the same for its chats. Each follows your own level until you choose
otherwise. You can also pause all notifications for a while, or set quiet hours
each day. You aren't notified about a chat you're looking at.

## Long chats

A bot rereads the chat every time it replies, and it can only hold so much in
mind at once. Think of it as the bot's **short-term memory**. When a chat gets
long, Sugabots makes room by compacting what the bot rereads. You don't have to
do anything, and nothing is deleted: everyone still sees the whole chat.

<CompactionDemo />

How it works:

- **It starts early.** Once a chat fills most of the bot's short-term memory,
  Sugabots starts compacting it, while there's still plenty of room to keep
  chatting.
- **It happens in the background.** Nobody waits for it, and nothing shows in
  the chat.
- **Recent messages stay whole.** The bot keeps rereading the latest messages
  word for word. The ones before them become a condensed account of what was
  decided, asked for and promised, what's still open, and key details like
  dates and prices, along with who said each thing.
- **The condensed account carries forward.** When the chat fills up again, the
  new one builds on the last.
- **The bot can look further back.** The condensed account can't hold everything. When the
  bot needs a detail it doesn't have, it searches the earlier messages.

To see how full a bot's short-term memory is, and when it was last compacted,
open the chat's details panel.
