The first time you sign in, Sugabots walks you through five short steps. Only
an admin can finish them, and whoever creates a workspace is its owner.

## The five steps

<Steps>

1. Name your workspace

   Something like "The Smiths' bots" or "Acme Support". You can rename it later
   in **Settings → General**.

2. Connect a model

   Bots need a model to think with. Pick **Anthropic**, **OpenAI**,
   **OpenRouter** or **Ollama**, then paste an API key, or give Ollama's server URL
   if it runs on your own machine. Then choose which of its models your bots
   use. You can switch on more, or add other providers, later in
   **Settings → Models**.

3. Meet your Personal Assistant

   Give it a colour, some eyes and a name. It's yours alone: it helps you find
   your way round the workspace and points you to the right bot. You can
   [make more bots](/docs/bots) for particular jobs once you're in.

4. Bring your friends

   Type in some email addresses and Sugabots sends each person an invite. They
   join as members. Invites expire after two days, and you can **Resend** them
   from **Settings → Members**. Or **Skip for now**.

   <Tip title="Sharing with others">

   If you want to share Sugabots with others, you'll need to get it
   [hosted](/docs/deploy-with-docker).
   Running it on your own computer, as in the Quickstart? Skip this step for
   now.

   </Tip>

5. Start chatting

   Your Personal Assistant is ready and says hello.

</Steps>

## After onboarding

<Tip title="Pick a behind-the-scenes model">

Sugabots uses a [model of its own](/docs/models#behind-the-scenes-model) to
summarise and title conversations. It starts on the model you picked during
setup. A fast, cheap model is best, so you may want to change it in
**Settings → Models**, under **Default → System bots use**.

</Tip>

A few good next steps:

- [Make more bots](/docs/bots), each with its own job and model.
- [Create a project](/docs/projects) for a group, and invite people to it once
  your installation is hosted.
- [Plug in a connection](/docs/connections), like Linear or Notion, so a bot can
  work with your apps.
