title: Projects

A project is where a group works on something together: a team, a family, a
flat, a launch. It has its members and its bots, led by one **lead bot**, its
**chats**, where they talk, and the **files** made there. A project is made
of its chats, each about one piece of work, such as one bug, one trip or one
week's report, that lasts as long as members keep it going.

## Public and private

When you make a project, you choose who can find it:

- **Public** projects are listed in the project directory. Anyone in the
  workspace can find one and join it, but nobody is added without choosing to.
- **Private** projects are invitation only. Nobody sees them unless they're in
  them.

The owner and admins are in every project members made, public or private.

A project with a [private bot](/docs/bots#public-private-and-personal) in it
stays private until every private bot leaves it. If a private bot leads the
project, choose another lead before removing it.

## Make a project

Owners, admins and editors can make projects from the project directory. Give
it a name like "Support", a line saying what it's for, choose its lead bot, and
choose public or private. You join the project when you create it, and the
lead is its first bot.

The sidebar lists the projects you're in, one row each, as it lists bots.
Choose one to open it: its own sidebar takes the place of the list, with its
chats, its **Files** and **Automations**, and the way back to everything. A
new project has no chats until someone starts one.

## Members

Anyone in a project except viewers can invite members from the workspace, or
remove them, and anyone but the owner and admins can leave. The owner and
admins can't be removed from a project or leave it, because they're in every
one.

Viewers can join public projects, read and take part, but they can't invite or
remove anyone.

## Bots

Anyone in a project, except viewers, can add bots to it and take them out. Any
[public bot](/docs/bots#public-private-and-personal) can be added, and a
private bot you can talk to can be added to a private project. Personal bots
can't be added. A bot that's made private leaves every public project it was in.

Everyone in the project can talk to its bots in the project's chats, a private
bot included, and the bots can ask each other for help there. A private bot can
also lead a private project. Its own chats and files stay with its members.

## Lead bot

Every project has one **lead bot**, chosen from the bots that can be in it,
which works on
the project and answers its chats unless another is asked. Mention one or
more bots by handle and each of them answers at once, or reply to a bot's
message and that bot answers. A message that mentions only members is read by
the lead bot, if it has a model, which answers only if it can help and
otherwise reacts 👀. If the lead bot has no model, the message is posted
without a bot reply. Any other message the lead bot answers. See
[Chats](/docs/chats#who-answers).

The owner and admins can choose another lead bot in the project's settings.
The lead bot stays in the project until another is chosen: it can't be taken
out of it, deleted, or, in a public project, made private before then.

## Chats

Anyone in the project can start a chat with **New chat** in the project's
own sidebar, or beside the project's row in the sidebar, by writing its first
message, which is answered like any other (see
[who answers](/docs/chats#who-answers)). Everyone in the project sees
every chat, with all the project's members and bots in it.

The project's own sidebar lists its chats, the most recently active first,
and can search them or show only some. On a phone, opening the project
shows its chat list. On a wider screen it opens the newest chat or, if there
are none, offers to start one.

Anyone in the project except viewers can mark a chat resolved once its work is finished, which
moves it to the Resolved list at the foot, where it can be reopened. The
owner and admins can rename a chat.

## Files

A project's **Files** are the ones uploaded or made in its chats. Everyone in the project can open them. The owner and admins can delete
them.

## Computer

A project has one online **computer**, a machine its bots run commands on,
keep files on and browse from while they work in its chats. It's made the
first time one of its bots needs it, kept between chats, and put to sleep
when idle. A bot working in this project's chats uses this computer, not its own,
so everything done for the project stays on the project's machine. Each bot
keeps a home folder of its own there, and each chat a folder of its own.

The bot's creator, the workspace's owner or an admin turns on **Computer use**
in the bot's settings. The workspace also needs an enabled computer provider,
which its owner and admins configure.

Everyone in the project can see how its computer stands and watch a bot's
desktop on it, and anyone but viewers can click and type there. The owner and
admins choose which internet addresses its computer may reach beyond the
workspace's, reset or upgrade it, and approve requests to add to the
project's allowed hosts.

## Connectors

A project has its own [connectors](/docs/connectors): the services and APIs its
bots work with, and at most one GitHub app. A bot working in the project's
chats uses the project's connectors, and those of its own that the
workspace's owner and admins have [turned on](/docs/connectors#a-bots-own-connectors-in-a-project)
there. If a project connector and a bot's own connector have the same
tool-name prefix, the project's connector takes precedence.
The owner and admins add and manage project connectors, and everyone in the
project can see which ones it has.

## Automations

A project has its own [automations](/docs/automations): work its lead bot does
on its own, on a timetable or when something calls it, posting in the
automation's feed, on the project's computer and with its connectors. The owner and admins
make and change them; everyone in the project can see them and their runs, and
anyone but viewers can choose **Start a run**.

## Rename or delete

The owner and admins can rename a project, change what it's for, and delete it.

<Careful title="Deleting a project">

**Delete project** removes its chats, files, connectors and automations,
and destroys its computer with everything on it. Its bots stay in the workspace. It can't be undone.

</Careful>
