A project is where a group works on something together: a team, a family, a
flat, a launch. It has its people and its bots, led by one **lead bot**, its
**threads**, where they talk, and the **files** made there. A project is made
of its threads, each one conversation, such as one bug, one trip or one week's
report, that lasts as long as people keep it going.

<ProjectMap />

## Public and private

When you make a project, you choose who can find it:

- **Public** projects are listed in the project directory. Anyone in the
  workspace can find one and join it, but nobody is added without choosing to.
- **Private** projects are invitation only. Nobody sees them unless they're in
  them.

The owner and admins are in every project people made, public or private.

## Make a project

Owners, admins and members can make projects from the project directory. Give
it a name like "Support", a line saying what it's for, choose its lead bot, and
choose public or private. You're its first member, and the lead bot its first
bot.

The sidebar lists the projects you're in, one row each, as it lists bots.
Choose one to open it: its own sidebar takes the place of the list, with its
threads, its **Files** and **Automations**, and the way back to everything. A
new project has no threads until someone starts one.

## People

Anyone in a project except viewers can invite people from the workspace, or
remove them, and anyone but the owner and admins can leave. The owner and
admins can't be removed from a project or leave it, because they're in every
one.

Viewers can join public projects, read and take part, but they can't invite or
remove anyone.

## Bots

Anyone in a project, except viewers, can add bots to it and take them out. Only
[public bots](/docs/bots#public-and-private) can be added, and a bot that's
made private leaves every project people made that it was in.

## Lead bot

Every project has one **lead bot**, chosen from the public bots, which works on
the project and answers its threads unless another is asked. Mention one or
more bots by handle and each of them answers at once, or reply to a bot's
message and that bot answers. A message that mentions only people is read by
the lead bot, if it has a model, which answers only if it can help and
otherwise reacts 👀. If the lead bot has no model, the message is posted
without a bot reply. Any other message the lead bot answers. See
[Conversations](/docs/conversations#who-answers).

The owner and admins can choose another lead bot in the project's settings.
The lead bot stays in the project until another is chosen: it can't be taken
out of it, made private or deleted before then. A project made before projects
had lead bots, which had no bots then, asks for one when it's opened.

## Threads

Anyone in the project can start a thread with **New thread** in the project's
own sidebar, or beside the project's row in the sidebar, by writing its first
message, which is answered like any other (see
[who answers](/docs/conversations#who-answers)). Everyone in the project sees
every thread, with all the project's people and bots in it.

The project's own sidebar lists its threads, the most recently active first,
and can search them or show only some. Opening the project opens its newest
thread, or, while it has none, starts one.

Anyone in a thread can mark it done once its work is finished. The owner and
admins can rename a thread, or archive it, which takes it off the list.

## Files

A project's **Files** are the ones uploaded or made in its threads. Everyone in the project can open them. The owner and admins can delete
them.

## Computer

A project has one online **computer**, a machine its bots run commands on,
keep files on and browse from while they work in its threads. It's made the
first time one of its bots needs it, kept between threads, and paused when
idle. A bot working in this project's threads uses this computer, not its own,
so everything done for the project stays on the project's machine. Each bot
keeps a home folder of its own there, and each thread a folder of its own.

A bot uses a computer only if its creator or an admin let it, in the bot's
settings, and only while the workspace has a computer provider set up.

Everyone in the project can see how its computer stands and watch a bot's
desktop on it, and anyone but viewers can click and type there. The owner and
admins, who can change the project, choose which internet addresses its
computer may reach beyond the workspace's, reset or upgrade it, and allow its
bots' requests to reach more.

## Connections

A project has its own [connections](/docs/connections): the apps and APIs its
bots work with, and at most one GitHub app. A bot working in the project's
threads uses the project's connections and its own; where both have one with
the same name, the project's is used. The owner and admins add and manage
them, and everyone in the project can see which ones it has.

## Automations

A project has its own [automations](/docs/automations): work its lead bot does
on its own, on a timetable or when something calls it, posting in the
automation's feed, on the project's computer and with its connections. The owner and admins
make and change them; everyone in the project can see them and their runs, and
anyone but viewers can run one now.

## Rename or delete

The owner and admins can rename a project, change what it's for, and delete it.

<Careful title="Deleting a project">

**Delete project** removes its threads, files, connections and automations,
and destroys its computer with everything on it. Its bots stay in the workspace. It can't be undone.

</Careful>
