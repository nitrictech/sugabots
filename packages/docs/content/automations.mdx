title: Automations
description: Work a project's lead bot does on its own, on its routines or whenever something calls it.

An automation is work a project's [lead bot](/docs/projects) does on its own:
"every weekday at 8, sort the inbox" or "when the shop gets an order, draft a
thank-you". The lead bot does it without being asked, in a thread of the
project, on the project's computer and with the project's
[connections](/docs/connections) as well as its own. It posts what it found in
the **automation feed**, the automation's own conversation in the project,
where its runs are listed too.

A bot's own automations are those of its hidden default project, which it
leads, so they run on its own computer with its own connections. They're in the
bot's **Automations**.

Whoever may change the project makes and changes its automations, and switches
them on or off: for the own automations of a bot people made, its creator, the
owner and admins; for a project people made, the owner and admins. Anyone in the
project, or who can talk to the bot, can see them and, except viewers, choose
**Run now**.

A [Personal Assistant](/docs/bots#your-personal-assistant)'s automations are its person's,
whatever their role, so a viewer manages their own assistant's too: they make
and change them, switch them on or off, choose **Run now**, and answer the
approvals their runs ask for. Nobody else sees them, not even the owner or an
admin.

## Who runs it

Each run is done by the project's lead bot when it starts. Choose another lead,
and the next run is that bot's.

## Make an automation

Choose **New automation**, then fill in:

- **Instructions**: what to do each time, written like you'd ask in a thread.
- **Routines**: when it runs on a timetable. Choose **Add routine** for each
  one.
- **Triggers**: events that start it. Choose **Add trigger** for each one: a
  **Webhook**, or **GitHub** when the project has a
  [GitHub app](/docs/connections).

An automation can have several of each, such as two routines and a webhook, and
one with none runs only when someone chooses **Run now**.

## Where the result goes

When a run finishes, the bot's last reply is posted in the automation feed,
where anyone in the project can answer it and carry on. There the lead bot
answers every message, unless it mentions only people; mentioning another bot
doesn't bring it in. Any file it wrote during the run is shown under that reply, at the version the run
left it at. The bot is told its last reply goes there, so it writes that reply
for you: a morning check-in, the week's sales, a reminder.

When something needs a person to act or decide, the bot mentions them, which
notifies them. Unless the instructions say who, that's whoever made the
automation and whoever started the run.

When there's nothing worth telling anyone, such as an order that needed no
thank-you, the bot posts nothing, and the run shows it had nothing to report.
You can still open the run to see what it did.

## Routines

A routine repeats **Daily**, **Weekly** or **Monthly**, every day, week or month or
every few: weekly on the days you pick, such as Monday to Friday, and monthly on
one day of the month or its last day. On each of those days it runs at one time
of your choosing, or at several. Have a go:

<RoutineBuilder />

A routine uses the timezone of whoever created the automation, or the
workspace's when a bot set it up. If Sugabots was down when a run was due, it
does one catch-up run rather than all the missed ones, and an automation never
runs twice at the same time.

## Ask the bot

Whoever manages a project's automations can also ask a bot in one of its
threads: "check in with me every morning at 9" or "move the inbox sweep to 10".
In its own threads, that's the bot's own automations; in a project's thread, the
project's, which the project's lead bot runs. It sets up, changes or pauses
their routines, in the workspace's timezone, and links to the automation in its
reply. Once an automation has more than one routine or trigger, its routine is
changed in settings. Anyone in the project can ask what automations it has.
Webhooks, and deleting an automation, stay in **Automations**.

Turn **Manage its automations** off in a bot's **Tools** to stop it.

## When something calls it

A **Webhook** trigger runs its automation whenever its address is called, so other apps can set
the project's lead bot to work. Each webhook has an address and a secret of its own; open it from the
automation's **Triggers** to see them. The secret stays hidden until **Show**.

<Careful title="Changing the secret">

The old secret stops working as soon as a new one is saved, so update the app
that calls the address straight away.

</Careful>

Call the address with a JSON body and a `Content-Type` of `application/json`, up to
64 KiB. The bot gets the body along with its instructions, marked as data from outside
so it isn't taken as orders. The caller proves it holds the secret the way the
webhook's **Source** and **Auth** say:

- **GitHub**, **Stripe**, **Slack** or **Linear**: paste the signing secret the
  sender made, and add the address in the sender, which signs each call itself.
  Slack's address check is answered for you.
- **Generic**, for any other sender, with one **Auth**:
  - **HMAC**: the sender signs the raw body with the secret and sends the digest in
    the **Header** you name. Pick SHA-1, SHA-256 or SHA-512, and hex or base64. A
    leading `sha256=` is fine.
  - **Bearer**: `Authorization: Bearer <secret>`.
  - **API key**: the secret in the **Header** you name.
  - **Basic**: HTTP Basic auth with the **Username** you set and the secret as the
    password.

The secret goes in a header, never in the address. A secret you make up needs at
least 16 characters. A webhook with no secret is run by anyone who has its address.

For example, with **Bearer** auth:

```sh
curl -X POST "https://sugabots.example.com/api/hooks/triggers/<trigger id>" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer <secret>" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: order-1042" \
  -d '{"order": 1042, "customer": "Sam"}'
```

A sender that signs, such as Sentry, needs no custom headers. Choose **Generic**,
**HMAC**, SHA-256 and hex, set the **Header** to `Sentry-Hook-Signature`, and
paste the integration's client secret as the secret.

Some senders call about everything, such as every change to every issue. In the
webhook trigger's **Filter** group, choose **Add condition** to run the automation
only for calls you want. Without conditions, every authenticated call can run it.

Each condition has a **JSON path**, with keys joined by dots, and one or more
**Accepted value** fields. Choose **Add value** for another alternative, then
**Save condition**. Every condition must match, but any accepted value within a
condition is enough. **Cancel condition** discards that edit. If a save fails,
the draft stays available to retry. When adding a webhook trigger, save or cancel
its condition before choosing **Add trigger**.

Values match exactly: commas, spaces and line breaks belong to the value rather
than separating alternatives. An empty value matches an empty string. Numbers
and booleans are compared as text, such as `2` and `false`. A missing path, an
object or `null` doesn't match a value.

Where a path crosses a list, any matching item counts. For example,
`data.labels.name` with the accepted value `10x` matches an issue that has that
label among others. Each condition searches the list independently.

For example, to run only when Linear creates an issue labelled `10x`, save these
three conditions:

- **JSON path** `type`, **Accepted value** `Issue`
- **JSON path** `action`, **Accepted value** `create`
- **JSON path** `data.labels.name`, **Accepted value** `10x`

Linear sends `update` when a label is added to an existing issue, so these
conditions don't match it. Accepting `update` would also match other changes to
an issue that already has the label; these conditions don't test what changed.

A filter allows at most 10 conditions, each with at most 20 accepted values.
Paths and values can each be at most 200 characters. Remove every condition to
stop filtering calls. Changing authentication or the delivery ID header keeps
the filter, and a copied automation keeps its filter too.

A call is answered with `202` and the run's `runId` as soon as the run is queued,
without waiting for the bot. Otherwise:

- `401` if the secret or signature is wrong, or the webhook is off or gone.
- `200` with `{"ignored": true}` if the call is genuine but its filter turns it away. No run
  starts, and the call isn't recorded.
- `400` if the body isn't JSON or the delivery id is over 200 characters.
- `413` if the body is over 64 KiB.

A delivery id makes a retry land on the run the first call started, which the
reply marks `"duplicate": true`. The same id sent with a different body is refused
with `409`. Where the id comes from depends on the **Source**:

- **GitHub** and **Linear**: their own delivery header, `X-GitHub-Delivery` or
  `Linear-Delivery`.
- **Slack** and **Stripe**: the `event_id` of a Slack event, or the `id` of a Stripe
  event, in the body.
- **Generic**: the header you name in **Delivery ID header**, `Idempotency-Key`
  until you change it. Clear it, and every call starts a run.

With no id, or when the header is missing, every call starts a run. A generic
webhook with an explicit header reads nothing else, so a sender that names its
deliveries another way, such as `Svix-Id` or `X-Shopify-Webhook-Id`, needs its
header named here. Authentication headers cannot be used as delivery IDs.

Existing webhooks and API-created webhooks without a delivery ID setting retain
**Automatic detection**: the first nonempty `Idempotency-Key`, `Linear-Delivery`,
`X-GitHub-Delivery`, `X-Shopify-Webhook-Id`, `Svix-Id`, or `Webhook-Id`, then the
body’s `event_id` or a Stripe event’s `id`. A header carrying the API key is
skipped. Unrelated edits keep this behavior until you choose a header or
**Every call runs**.

<Careful title="Sentry retries aren't deduplicated">

Sentry's `Request-ID` changes with every attempt, so a retry of a Sentry delivery
starts a second run, whatever **Delivery ID header** says. Leave the field blank
and let the bot spot the repeat from the alert itself.

</Careful>

## Runs

Each run has a thread of its own with everything the bot did, and shows in the
automation feed as a line like "Morning inbox sweep ran". A posted
result links back to its run, under the message. A run that fails or is
stopped posts nothing; its line says what happened. If a run needs an
[approval](/docs/tools-and-approvals), only whoever manages the project's
automations can answer it: for the own automations of a bot people made, its
creator, the owner or an admin; for a Personal Assistant's, its person, even a
viewer.
